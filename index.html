    03Coach’s notes

    Easy really means easy

    Your current easy pace of 6:00–6:30 is on the quick side for your fitness. Keeping easy and long runs in the ranges shown above is what lets you absorb the hard Wednesday sessions and protects your calves and shins.

    Calves and shins

    • The plan builds gradually, with a lighter week every few weeks. Don’t add extra distance on top.
    • Prioritise calf raises (straight and bent knee) and tibialis raises in your strength work, and keep heavy leg sessions away from the day before Wednesday’s session or the long run.
    • If your calves or shins feel tight after a long run, swap Sunday for 30–40 minutes on a bike or cross-trainer.
    • Pain on a specific spot of the shin bone, or pain that gets worse as you run, is a stop signal. Take 2–3 days off, and see a physio if it lasts more than a week.
    • Keep an eye on shoe mileage; most trainers need replacing somewhere around 600–800 km.

    When life gets in the way

    • Missed a Wednesday? Let it go. Don’t squeeze it into another day.
    • Missed a Saturday long run? Do it on Sunday instead of the easy run, never both.
    • Ill or injured for more than a week? Repeat the previous week before moving on.

    Fuelling practice

    From Week 7, take a gel or similar every 30–40 minutes on any run over 90 minutes. Use the long runs to rehearse your race-morning breakfast and kit too. Check which gels the event offers on course and practise with those, or plan to carry your own.

    The optional fourth run

    From Week 11 there’s an optional easy run. Any day works, ideally Monday or Tuesday. Avoid Friday, the day before the long run. Skipping it is fine; it’s a bonus, not a requirement.

    Race day

    Sub-4 is 5:41 per km, but GPS usually measures a little long and you won’t run perfect lines, so aim for 5:35–5:38 on your watch. Go out at the slow end for the first 5 km. If you feel good at 32 km, that’s when you can push.
